# flurr — competitor research & positioning

Quick scan of the Indian handmade-crochet market (Oct 2026) to make sure flurr doesn't
read like another Shopify crochet shop. This file is for the team, not shipped as a
public page.

## Who else is in this space

| Brand | What they do | What their site feels like |
|---|---|---|
| mindori.in | Crochet bouquets | "Ready to ship" vs "made to order in small batches" split; otherwise a standard Shopify catalogue |
| thecrochetparlour.com (Chennai) | Amigurumi, bouquets, keychains, bag charms | Dense category grid, busy product photography, stock-style badges |
| rdhruti.com | Amigurumi + accessories | Generic Shopify template, loud accent colour, little brand voice |
| Oma Studio | Plushies & home pieces | Catalogue-first, little narrative around the maker |
| Viola Vintage | Crochet accessories | Pastel palette but same grid-of-cards structure |
| A Trillion Smiles | Amigurumi gifting | Cute/kitsch visual language, busy layouts |
| Crafty Hook Studio | Mixed crochet goods | Template storefront, minimal story-telling |

## The pattern across almost all of them

- Busy product grids, many items at once, little breathing room.
- Loud, saturated colour choices (hot pink, bright yellow backgrounds) that fight the
  handmade material instead of letting the yarn and stitches read as the "colour."
- Generic Shopify/WooCommerce visual templates — stock badges ("Bestseller," "Only 2
  left"), carousel heroes, countdown-style urgency.
- Products presented as inventory ("Giraffe Amigurumi — ₹999") rather than as pieces
  with a name, a maker, or a story.
- Photography is serviceable but rarely slow, tactile or natural-light — mostly flat
  product-on-white shots.

## Where flurr can differentiate

1. **A maker's studio, not a storefront.** flurr has one founder (Samiksha) and a small,
   recognisable visual identity already (sage green, undyed linen, a kraft hang-tag).
   Lean into that: the site should feel like stepping into her workspace, not browsing
   a catalogue.
2. **Named pieces with a story, not SKUs.** GIGI the Giraffe is already a character, not
   a product code. Every piece gets a name and a line of story — this is the single
   biggest lever flurr has that competitors don't use.
3. **"Flowers that never wilt."** None of the bouquet competitors (mindori, Trillion
   Smiles) frame crochet flowers as a permanence/gifting story this directly — most just
   sell them as decor. flurr can own this line.
4. **Made-to-order, stated plainly, without urgency tactics.** Competitors use
   "ready to ship" vs "made to order" as an inventory distinction. flurr has no
   inventory at all yet — so instead of faux scarcity, be honest and calm about it:
   every piece is made by hand, for you, after you reach out. No countdowns, no stock
   badges, no "only 2 left."
5. **Quiet, tactile design over busy grids.** Where competitors cram many products into
   tight grids with loud colours, flurr can commit to one calm palette (sage + linen +
   a single warm yellow accent from GIGI), generous whitespace, a real linen texture
   underfoot, and a slow scroll rhythm — closer to an editorial portfolio than an
   e-commerce template.
6. **DM-first ordering, designed on purpose.** Competitors bolt WhatsApp/DM ordering
   onto a Shopify cart as an afterthought. flurr has no cart at all — ordering via
   Instagram DM or WhatsApp is the whole model, so the site should make that feel like
   a feature (a personal conversation with the maker) rather than a workaround.

## Net position

flurr is the slow, personal, maker-led alternative in a category of loud template
storefronts: fewer pieces, each with a name and a story, made by one pair of hands,
ordered through a conversation rather than a cart.
